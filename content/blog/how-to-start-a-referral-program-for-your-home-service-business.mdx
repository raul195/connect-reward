You already know that your best customers come from referrals.

They close faster. They trust you before the first phone call. And they cost a fraction of what you'd spend on ads to acquire.

But here's the thing — most home service businesses don't have a *system* for generating referrals. They rely on hope. Hope that happy customers will mention them to a neighbor. Hope that a one-time $50 bonus was enough motivation to keep someone referring.

Hope isn't a strategy. **A referral program is.**

---

## What makes a referral program actually work?

Not all referral programs are created equal. The ones that generate consistent results share three qualities:

**They're effortless for customers.** If someone has to call you, fill out a long form, or remember a code — they won't do it. Submitting a referral should take less than a minute.

**They reward more than once.** A one-time cash bonus gets deposited and forgotten. A points-based system with leaderboards, badges, and a rewards catalog keeps customers coming back month after month.

**They run themselves.** You shouldn't be tracking referrals in a spreadsheet or manually sending bonus checks. Automation handles the busywork so you can focus on doing great work.

## The 5-step setup

### 1. Figure out what a referral is worth to you

Start with your margins — not your revenue.

If your average job brings in $5,000 in profit, offering 5–10% of that as a referral reward ($250–$500) is a strong incentive that keeps you solidly profitable. Remember: a referred customer costs you almost nothing in marketing spend.

> **Quick math:** A $25,000 solar install with a 30% margin = $7,500 profit. A 7% referral reward = **$525 per referral.** That's less than 3% of the total job value — and you got a pre-sold customer out of it.

### 2. Build a rewards catalog people actually want

Give your customers something to work toward. Gift cards, branded merch, premium prizes — the key is offering rewards at different point levels so there's always a next goal.

A tip that might surprise you: **don't just offer cash equivalents.** A customer who *earns* a $100 Amazon gift card through referrals remembers your company differently than one who received a $100 check. The experience of choosing and redeeming a reward creates a stronger emotional connection.

### 3. Invite the customers you already have

You don't need to wait for new customers. You already have a base of happy people who finished their project and loved the result.

Upload your customer list and send them a branded welcome email inviting them to join your rewards program. The best time to do this? **Right after you've completed their job**, when satisfaction is at its peak.

### 4. Make it stupidly easy to refer

Customers should be able to submit a referral in under a minute — open the portal, type a name and phone number, hit submit. Done.

You get notified instantly when a new referral comes in. No chasing, no phone tag, no missed opportunities.

### 5. Close the loop (this is the one everyone misses)

When a referral turns into a job, approve it. Points get awarded automatically. The customer gets notified. They can see their balance grow and their progress toward the next reward.

**If a customer never hears back about their referral, they won't send another one.** This step is what separates a referral program from a suggestion box.

## How much should you actually spend?

Here's a quick benchmark by industry:

| Industry | Avg Job | Margin | Reward Range |
|----------|---------|--------|-------------|
| Solar | $25,000 | 25–35% | $375–$750 |
| Roofing | $12,000 | 30–40% | $360–$480 |
| HVAC | $8,000 | 35–45% | $280–$360 |
| Windows | $6,000 | 30–40% | $180–$240 |
| Plumbing | $3,000 | 35–45% | $105–$135 |

All of these assume a 5–10% reward as a percentage of **profit** — not revenue. That distinction matters a lot.

## Why gamification changes everything

Points alone are fine. Points with gamification are *powerful.*

**Leaderboards** tap into friendly competition. When your customer sees they're #3 on the referral leaderboard, they want to be #1.

**Badges** create milestone moments. Earning the "Hat Trick" badge for 3 referrals feels like an achievement — because it is one.

**Tiers** build long-term engagement. Moving from Bronze to Silver to Gold unlocks better rewards and gives customers status. Nobody wants to drop a tier.

**Bonus multipliers** create urgency. A limited-time 2x points promotion drives a spike in referrals right when you need them.

The result? What used to be a forgettable cash handoff becomes an experience customers genuinely enjoy — and keep coming back to.

---

## Three mistakes that kill referral programs

**Making it complicated.** Ten-field forms, referral codes, terms and conditions nobody reads. Keep it simple: name, number, submit.

**Going silent.** If a customer refers someone and never hears what happened, that's the last referral you'll get from them. Automated status emails solve this completely.

**Only offering cash.** Cash gets deposited and forgotten. A rewards program with progress tracking and a catalog creates an ongoing relationship with your brand.
